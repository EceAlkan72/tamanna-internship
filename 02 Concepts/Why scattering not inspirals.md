# Why scattering, not inspirals?

(My framing for explaining the paper — context from the field, not a claim made in the paper.)

**Inspirals are messy to compare**
- Orbit shrinks continuously → conservative and dissipative physics mixed over many orbits.
- Waveform comparisons need time/phase alignment choices.
- Expensive; hard to isolate one effect.

**Scattering is clean**
- One encounter → one number, the [[Scattering angle]].
- Defined at infinity → gauge-invariant; NR and analytics compare without coordinate ambiguity.
- Like Rutherford scattering: $\chi(b)$ at fixed $E$ ↔ the potential. Small $b$ probes short-range physics ([[Scalar potential w_scalar]], [[Tidal potential w_tid]]); large $b$ ≈ BH.
- The extracted [[Effective-one-body]] potential $w$ is the same object used for bound orbits → transferable to GW templates.

**Caveat**: $\chi$ also includes radiation effects; $w^{\rm BH}$ is an *effective* potential absorbing radiation reaction. Scalar interaction has a small non-conservative piece when $\Phi_1\ne\Phi_2$ (negligible here).
